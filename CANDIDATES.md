# Candidates — One Line to Your Younger Self

Judged pairwise against the brief, in the brief's own order:
1. a line told to the younger self, not a slogan for nobody;
2. only this person's older self could say it, drawn from a life actually lived;
3. lands in one breath, real voice, no explaining;
4. a turn, an image, or a small surprise that stays after one reading;
5. honest and specific enough to sting a little, hard-won or warm, not sentimental.

## Candidate A
> Kid, the paint never really washes off your hands — neither do the years, so spend them building something that's yours.

## Candidate B
> You will paint a thousand walls for other people before you build a single thing of your own — start with the wall in front of you.

## Pairwise: A vs B
- (1) Tie — both speak straight to the younger self.
- (2) B edges it — "a thousand walls for other people" is a working life, not just a physical detail.
- (3) A is one flowing breath; B is two beats joined by a dash.
- (4) A's paint → years parallel is the cleaner surprise.
- (5) B stings more.

First difference is (2), so B wins — but B's turn is slightly instructional
("start with..."), which drifts toward motto. The winner below keeps B's
lived specificity and A's image, in one breath.

## Winner (in main.ts)
> Kid, you'll paint a thousand walls for other people, and the paint never washes off your hands — so make the next wall yours.

Why it wins: criterion (2) is the strongest of the three — painting walls for
other people is the entrant's actual day job, and the paint on his hands is
the physical proof. "Make the next wall yours" is the turn and the sting at
once: the grind (quests, hackathons, bounties) is exactly him trying to build
something of his own. One sentence, one breath, no explaining.
